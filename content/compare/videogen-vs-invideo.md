## How we evaluated this

This is a research-based comparison, without first-hand test footage of our own. We read both pricing pages and terms, VideoGen's rate card and help centre, and both Trustpilot profiles, including the 20 latest one-star reviews for each. Checked November 5, 2026.

## The short answer

VideoGen looks like the better deal for most people, because its entry plans give you more for less. InVideo has one advantage worth caring about: a real refund window. Neither has a user reputation we'd call comfortable, so start monthly with either one.

## Choose VideoGen if

- You want more footage per dollar at entry level.
- You're starting from slides, scripts or voiceovers.
- You need an API.

## Choose InVideo if

- A seven-day refund window matters to you.
- You want to prompt an agent and let it build the video.
- You need guest seats on a team plan.

## The real differences

**Entry price.** VideoGen Pro is $29 monthly or $19 yearly. InVideo Starter is $20 monthly or $10 yearly, which looks cheaper, but it has just 400 credits and no access to Seedance 2.5. InVideo's closer equivalent, Plus, is $60 monthly or $30 yearly.

**Refunds.** VideoGen's terms say all purchases are non-refundable. InVideo's terms allow a refund within seven days of purchase or renewal, provided you haven't used credits or premium features. That condition limits it, since you can't test much without spending credits.

**Rollover.** Both reset plan credits monthly. VideoGen sells extra credits that never expire. One InVideo reviewer reported bought credits expiring, which we couldn't confirm.

**Starting points.** VideoGen lists more ways to start, from storyboards to slides. Some InVideo complaints involve an agent spending credits while it works, so review what it plans before you approve it.

Credits aren't comparable between these tools, so ignore the raw numbers. A rough footage comparison works better:

- **VideoGen Pro:** about 4 minutes of generative video at Standard quality, by its own estimate.
- **InVideo Plus:** about 35 five-second Seedance 2.5 clips at 720p, or just under 3 minutes.

So VideoGen's estimate is higher, at a lower monthly price. The models and quality differ, though, so treat this as a guide. At yearly prices, the gap closes: $19 against $30 on these plans.

Top-up costs at VideoGen are $10 per 1,000 credits. High quality there uses 100 credits per second, which would burn through Pro's allowance in 60 seconds. Whichever tool you choose, your quality setting matters more than your plan name.

## What users complain about in each

Both profiles have many five-star reviews and a large one-star group, and both have few replies from the company.

| | VideoGen | InVideo |
|---|---|---|
| Trustpilot score | 2.7 | 1.8 |
| Reviews | 108 | 1,028 |
| One-star share | 35% | 32% |
| Reviews in last 12 months | 12 | 115 |
| Replies to negative reviews | None shown as a pattern | 15% |

InVideo's percentages look better than its 1.8 score suggests, since Trustpilot weighs recent reviews and other factors. A separate profile for its AI product shows 2.9 from 910 reviews, which we didn't read.

The complaints differ, and that's useful:

- **VideoGen:** billing dominates. About 12 of 20 mention charges after cancelling, trouble cancelling or refused refunds.
- **InVideo:** wasted credits dominate. 11 of 20 say credits ran out without a usable video, and some describe a month's credits vanishing on one short project.

Put simply, VideoGen's reviewers worry about getting out, and InVideo's worry about getting anything out.

## Switching

Neither tool locks in your source material. Export what you've made, keep your scripts and decks somewhere else, and cancel before renewal. Check rights first: both let you use outputs commercially, but stock inside a video can carry its own terms. If you're leaving, save your finished work before the plan ends.

## Who should skip both

If you already film yourself and only need captions and tighter edits, you don't need a generative tool at all. Look at [Submagic](/tools/submagic). If you're cutting long recordings into shorts, [OpusClip](/tools/opusclip) is built for that job and bills in a far simpler way.

## Verdict

Pick VideoGen for narrated explainers and slide-based videos, on Pro with monthly billing. It offers more usable video at entry level, and its credit rules are published clearly.

Pick InVideo only if you value its refund window or want its agent workflow. Make sure you test it on a short project before spending a month's credits.

If avatars are your main need, skip both and look at [Synthesia](/tools/synthesia).

## FAQ

### Is VideoGen cheaper than InVideo?

At entry level on monthly billing, for comparable output, yes. InVideo's $10 yearly Starter is cheaper in dollars, but it has far fewer credits.

### Which has better reviews?

VideoGen scores higher on Trustpilot, at 2.7 against 1.8, but it has far fewer recent reviews. Neither is good.

### Can I get a refund from either?

InVideo's terms offer one within seven days if you haven't used credits. VideoGen's terms say purchases are non-refundable.

### Which should I use for AI avatars?

Neither is our first choice. Synthesia is built around avatars and has a stronger record.
