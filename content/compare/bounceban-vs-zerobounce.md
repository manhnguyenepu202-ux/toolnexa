## How we evaluated this

This is a research-based comparison, without verification runs of our own. We read both pricing pages, BounceBan's help centre, terms and privacy notice, ZeroBounce's catch-all documentation and Trustpilot profile, and two 2026 benchmarks. Checked November 5, 2026.

One warning before any numbers: ZeroBounce ran one of those benchmarks and Allegrow, a rival to both, ran the other. We found no neutral test.

## The one question that decides it

What's on your list? If it's B2B contacts at company domains, mostly for outbound, start with BounceBan. If it's a marketing list with lots of personal addresses, or you want your verifier and deliverability tools under one login, start with ZeroBounce. The price gap makes that first test cheap either way.

## The real differences

**Scope.** BounceBan is a verifier and nothing else. ZeroBounce also sells email finding, activity data, warm-up, inbox placement testing, blacklist monitoring and DMARC monitoring. You pay more, and you get more.

**Catch-all approach.** Both now try to resolve catch-all addresses instead of giving up. BounceBan built its product around this. ZeroBounce does it in standard validation, with Verify+ for further checks on some addresses, and returns anything left as catch-all. ZeroBounce's page says its scoring doesn't confirm a catch-all mailbox, so don't confuse the two.

**Consumer addresses.** This is the clearest gap. In ZeroBounce's own benchmark, BounceBan hit only 55.25% on Yahoo and 82.65% across free domains. Allow for who ran the test, but we wouldn't ignore a gap that wide.

**Trust and paperwork.** ZeroBounce has years of public reviews, with a Trustpilot profile dating from 2017. BounceBan's terms and privacy notice date from November 2022, and they still cite the defunct Privacy Shield. Larger buyers should raise this with both vendors, but BounceBan has more to answer.

## The benchmark conflict

| Test | Run by | BounceBan | ZeroBounce |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10,000 catch-all records | Allegrow | 460 of 500 people found, 9 false valid, 1 unresolved | 467 to 470 found, 9 to 25 false valid, 0 to 90 unresolved |
| 9,901 hard addresses | ZeroBounce | 86.06% accurate | 98.02% accurate |
| Business domains only | ZeroBounce | 96.66% accurate | Not compared here |

Allegrow's result is close, and ZeroBounce's own result isn't. Different addresses and different definitions of truth explain part of that. ZeroBounce's engineers settled disputed addresses themselves, and Allegrow didn't confirm that the mailboxes of its 500 people existed.

Our reading: on catch-all B2B data the two look close, and ZeroBounce is ahead on the wider mix. We wouldn't quote either percentage as fact.

## Pricing head-to-head

| | BounceBan | ZeroBounce |
|---|---|---|
| Entry paid option | $40 for 10,000 credits | $39 for 2,000 credits |
| Subscription | $34 for 10,000 | $99/mo, 10,000 minimum |
| Cost per 1,000 at entry | $3.40 to $4.00 | $9.90 to $19.50 |
| Free use | Unlimited single checks, fair use applies | 100 credits a month |
| Unknown results | Unclear, see below | Free |
| Credit expiry | Never | Never with ZeroBounce ONE, otherwise rules vary |
| Refunds | Non-refundable, per terms | Not confirmed |

At 10,000 addresses, BounceBan costs less than half of ZeroBounce's subscription. Third-party guides put ZeroBounce's pay-as-you-go price for 10,000 between $60 and $130, and they disagree, so run its calculator yourself.

ZeroBounce is clearer about charges: its pricing page says unknown results are free. BounceBan's docs only say "successful" verifications cost a credit, and Allegrow reports that bulk jobs refund unknown results while risky and undeliverable results still cost one. In practice BounceBan returns very few unknowns, so this matters less than it sounds.

Scaling up helps BounceBan further. It falls to $2.55 per 1,000 at 100,000 on subscription, and $1.45 at a million. We couldn't read ZeroBounce's calculator at those volumes.

## What users complain about in each

**ZeroBounce** has a strong Trustpilot score of 4.8 from 2,138 reviews, with just 2% one-star, though only 64 reviews came in the last 12 months. Of the first 20 one-star reviews, billing and credits led with 8, including a cost surprise on Email Finder. Accuracy came next with 6, although five of those date from 2020 or earlier.

**BounceBan** has no Trustpilot profile we could find. G2 sits around 4.8 from about 300 reviews, which we saw second-hand. Reported gripes cover pricing for small lists, paying for bad addresses, slower processing on large lists, and thin analytics.

## Switching

There's almost nothing to migrate. Both take CSV uploads and offer an API. The practical step is a side-by-side run. Take 2,000 addresses you plan to mail, verify them in both, and send to the ones they disagree on. Your bounces will tell you more than either benchmark. If you move your CRM cleaning, check each tool's integration first, as BounceBan's Sync is a separate subscription.

## Verdict

For outbound teams with catch-all-heavy B2B lists, BounceBan is the better buy. It's far cheaper, and in the one test built around catch-alls it stayed close. For marketing lists with consumer addresses, or companies that want fuller tooling and steadier paperwork, ZeroBounce is the safer pick, and we'd pay the extra.

That's why ZeroBounce scores higher overall on this site, even though BounceBan is the one we'd try first for cold outbound.

## FAQ

### Is BounceBan cheaper than ZeroBounce?

Yes, at the volumes we could confirm. BounceBan costs $34 to $40 for 10,000 checks, while ZeroBounce's subscription starts at $99 a month.

### Which is more accurate?

It depends on who you ask. ZeroBounce's benchmark favours ZeroBounce heavily, and Allegrow's shows them close on catch-alls. Neither test is neutral.

### Which is better for Gmail and Yahoo lists?

ZeroBounce, on the evidence we have. BounceBan's Yahoo result was its weakest in ZeroBounce's benchmark.

### Can I use both?

Yes. You could clean with one tool and pass its risky results to the other, though you'd pay twice for those addresses.
