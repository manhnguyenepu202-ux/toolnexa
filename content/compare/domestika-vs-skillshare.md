## How we evaluated this

This is a research-based comparison, and we didn't take courses on either platform. We read Domestika's Plus page, a sample course and its terms, plus both Trustpilot profiles, including the 20 latest one-star reviews for each. Skillshare's pricing pages didn't load for us, and Domestika's dollar prices didn't display, so most dollar figures come from third-party guides. Checked November 5, 2026.

## The short answer

These platforms sell different things. Buy from Domestika if you want one well-made course on a specific skill, and you can do that without any subscription. Choose Skillshare if you want to dip into lots of topics for one yearly price.

Avoid Domestika Plus unless you're sure you'll use it. Both platforms draw the same complaint about surprise charges, so whichever you pick, set a calendar reminder.

## Choose Domestika if

- You know the skill you want to learn.
- You'd like to keep the course after you've paid.
- You want a 14-day refund window on what you buy.
- Audio or subtitles in other languages matter to you.

## Choose Skillshare if

- You want to sample many creative subjects.
- You'd take several short classes a month.
- You're fine losing access when you stop paying.

## The real differences

**Ownership.** This is the deciding point. A course bought on Domestika stays yours, even if you cancel Plus. Skillshare is a library, so when the subscription ends, so does access to its classes.

**How you pay.** Domestika has two routes: single courses and the Plus subscription. Skillshare is reported as subscription only, with a 7-day trial. That gives Domestika the lower-risk option, as long as you don't add Plus by accident.

**Production.** Domestika makes its courses in-house, and the sample we read ran to 15 lessons and about 2.5 hours. Skillshare's library is larger, reported at over 30,000 classes, and teacher quality varies more because it's a much broader catalog.

**Language.** Domestika's sample course offered audio in eight languages. That's a real advantage for learners outside the English-speaking world.

| Eight courses in a year | About $160 | About $168 |

This uses the $20 top of the reported sale range, so it's a cautious estimate. Two things stand out. For fewer than eight courses a year, buying single Domestika courses costs less than subscribing to Skillshare, and you keep them. But for two years of subscription, Skillshare comes in about $158 cheaper, because Domestika's renewal doubles.

So if you're choosing between the two subscriptions, Skillshare is the better deal. If you're choosing between single courses and a subscription, single courses win for most people.

One-star reviews are a self-selected sample, so this shows what goes wrong, not how often. Still, the message is plain. Unhappy customers on both platforms didn't realise a trial or membership would turn into a full annual charge.

Domestika's complaints often begin with a cheap course, which is worth noting. People came for one purchase and say they ended up with a yearly membership. Skillshare's come more often from a trial or a renewal.

Skillshare's public replies to reviewers suggest no refunds on renewals, with a possible exception for a charge just after a trial if you ask within 48 hours. Domestika's terms offer no 14-day withdrawal on Plus, though mandatory rights in the EEA, UK and Switzerland still apply. So assume you won't get your money back, and if you're charged by mistake, contact them the same day.

## How to avoid the surprise charge

1. Read every line at checkout before you pay.
2. Check your account straight after buying to see whether a membership started.
3. If you start a trial, put the end date in your calendar, a few days early.
4. Don't rely on a reminder email, as many reviewers say none arrived.
5. Cancel right away if you only wanted the trial. Check that access continues until it ends before you do.

## Switching

Moving between them is simple, because there's no data to carry over. Cancel before your renewal date, and keep what you've bought on Domestika. Use any remaining Domestika credits first, because they end with your membership.

## Verdict

For a focused learner, Domestika's single courses are the best buy here, at low sale prices with no ongoing cost. For a curious learner who wants variety, Skillshare is the better subscription, with a steadier yearly price and better reviews. We wouldn't choose Domestika Plus over Skillshare unless you specifically want Domestika's courses to keep.

## FAQ

### Is Domestika or Skillshare cheaper?

For a few courses a year, Domestika's single courses are cheaper. As a subscription over two years, Skillshare is cheaper, at about $336 against about $494.

### Do you keep Skillshare classes?

No. Access ends when your subscription does. Domestika courses you've bought stay in your account.

### Which has better reviews?

Skillshare, at 4.0 on Trustpilot against Domestika's 3.6, though both draw complaints about surprise charges.

### Can I get a refund?

Domestika offers 14 days on course purchases, but not on Plus. Skillshare's replies suggest no refunds on renewals.
