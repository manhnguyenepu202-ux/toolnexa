## How we evaluated this

This is a research-based comparison, and we didn't run recoveries ourselves. We read both vendors' product pages, EaseUS's refund policy, and Digital Trends' published test from August 2024. CleverFiles' pricing page wasn't available to us, so some Disk Drill details are unconfirmed, and we flag them. Trustpilot and Capterra refused our browser. Checked November 5, 2026.

## The short answer

For most people with one emergency, EaseUS is the easier pick. Its free allowance is much bigger, its plans are clearly listed, and in the one independent test we trust, it did as well or better. Disk Drill makes more sense if you'd keep the tool around, or care most about price, but confirm the licence term before you pay.

## Choose EaseUS if

- You need to see more than a small sample before paying.
- You want to pay once for a single rescue.
- You want published plan prices and a written refund policy.

## Choose Disk Drill if

- You've got only small files to recover, or want a second opinion.
- You'd like one licence on several computers.
- The checkout confirms a lifetime licence at a lower price than EaseUS.

## The real differences

**Free allowance.** This matters most in a crisis. EaseUS advertises up to 2 GB of free recovery. Disk Drill's Windows page says 100 MB, while older third-party sources say 500 MB. Even at the higher figure, EaseUS gives you several times more, which can mean getting a whole folder of photos back without paying at all.

**Test results.** Digital Trends tried both in 2024. Both recovered hard-drive files in every test and restored a damaged thumb drive. On SSDs, EaseUS was the only one that restored files after a quick format, although the writer suspected that was luck. Neither got back files simply deleted from the SSD. So there's no clear winner on the disks most people have, and neither is a fix for SSD deletions.

**Licensing.** EaseUS offers One-Month, One-Year and Lifetime. Disk Drill's terms are murkier. Digital Trends reported $89 for Pro, plus $19 for lifetime upgrades, and said it installs on three computers. A later third-party source lists $89 as perpetual, while Disk Drill's own Windows page shows "$89.00 (year)" in a comparison table. Those can't all be right today.

**Refund clarity.** EaseUS publishes a 30-day policy, though it's narrow: technical failures only, after support has tried, and not if the product "has already been used successfully". We couldn't confirm Disk Drill's refund terms, so read them before paying.

**Business use.** EaseUS has a Technician edition for multiple PCs and servers, with RAID and bootable recovery. If you run a repair shop, that's a defined product to evaluate.

## What users say about each

EaseUS holds about 4.6 out of 5 on Capterra from around 411 reviews. Its Trustpilot figures conflict across search listings, so we won't quote one as current. Refund disputes are the most repeated complaint, including people refused despite the guarantee. Failed recoveries in complex cases come next.

We found less direct user data on Disk Drill. Digital Trends noted 4.4 stars from fewer than 300 Trustpilot ratings in 2024, which is both old and small. That's not proof of a worse product, only less evidence to judge it by.

## When neither is right

If a drive clicks, grinds or isn't detected, don't run either. Software scans put extra wear on a failing disk, and a recovery lab is the better first call when the data matters.

The same goes for expectations on SSDs. Once a file is deleted from an SSD that's in normal use, the odds are poor with any software. Both tools are best suited to hard drives and USB sticks that still work.

## Switching

You don't really switch between these, because neither holds your data. That's helpful. If one tool's scan misses your files, run the other tool's free scan before paying anything. Install on a different drive, avoid writing to the affected disk, and restore to somewhere else.

## Verdict

Start with EaseUS's free scan. It lets you see the most before you commit, and it may be all you need. If your files fit within the free allowance, you're finished. If you need to pay for a single rescue, One-Month is easy to justify. Try Disk Drill if EaseUS misses your files, or if you want a tool to keep and its checkout confirms a good lifetime price.

## FAQ

### Is EaseUS better than Disk Drill?

For a one-off rescue, we'd start with EaseUS, thanks to its bigger free allowance and clearer plans. Test results between them are close.

### Is Disk Drill a one-time purchase?

It's reported that way, but its own Windows page lists $89 per year in a table. Check the term at checkout.

EaseUS, at up to 2 GB advertised, against 100 MB on Disk Drill's Windows page.

Neither managed it in Digital Trends' test. Don't count on it.
