## The short answer

Most creators need just one or two of these, depending on what you make:

- **You film long videos or podcasts:** OpusClip to cut them into Shorts.
- **You film yourself talking:** Submagic for captions and quick edits.
- **You want AI to edit from instructions:** InVideo.
- **You turn scripts or slides into explainers:** VideoGen.
- **You publish in several languages:** Fliki.
- **You run faceless story channels:** Crayo.

## How we evaluated this

This is a research-based guide built on our individual reviews of each tool. We read pricing pages, refund terms and recent Trustpilot reviews for each. Checked November 2026.

## 1. OpusClip: best for clipping long videos

Upload a podcast, stream or long YouTube video and OpusClip picks the strongest moments, reframes them vertically, adds captions and scores each clip for virality.

- **From:** about $15 a month. Free plan adds a watermark.
- **Watch out for:** exports on the free plan expire quickly, and some users report charges after cancelling.

## 2. Submagic: best captions for talking heads

Submagic adds bold, animated captions, emojis, zooms and B-roll to short talking-head videos, the style that dominates Reels and TikTok.

- **From:** about $19 a month. No free plan.
- **Watch out for:** length limits per video by plan, and non-refundable fees.

Comparing the two? See [Submagic vs OpusClip](/compare/submagic-vs-opusclip).

## 3. InVideo: best all-in-one AI editor

InVideo's agent generates and edits video from your written instructions, combining stock footage, AI clips, voiceover and music.

- **From:** about $10 per seat a month billed yearly.
- **Watch out for:** credits can be spent on drafts you don't use, and plan credits expire. Ask the agent to confirm before generating.

## 4. VideoGen: best for script and slide videos

VideoGen turns a script, a slide deck or a voiceover into a finished video with visuals and captions. Good for explainers, courses and business updates.

- **From:** about $19 per user a month billed yearly.
- **Watch out for:** credits burn faster at higher quality, and purchases are non-refundable.

## 5. Fliki: best for multilingual voiceover videos

Fliki converts scripts and blog posts into narrated videos in 80+ languages, with avatars and stock media.

- **From:** about $21 a month billed yearly.
- **Watch out for:** credits don't roll over, and support is the top complaint in recent reviews.

See [Fliki vs InVideo](/compare/fliki-vs-invideo) for a head-to-head.

## 6. Crayo: best for faceless story videos

Crayo makes Reddit-story videos, fake text conversations and gameplay-background clips, a popular faceless format on TikTok and Shorts.

- **From:** about $16.58 a month billed yearly.
- **Watch out for:** all sales are final, and voiceover minutes cap how much you can make.

## How to avoid wasting money

1. **Start monthly**, then switch to yearly once you've used a tool for a month.
2. **Count your real output.** Credits and minute caps matter more than the headline price.
3. **Screenshot your cancellation**, as billing complaints are common across this category.
4. **Check commercial-use terms** if you monetise your channel or make client work.
5. **Keep editing time in mind.** The best tool is the one whose first draft needs the fewest fixes.

## FAQ

### What is the best AI tool to make YouTube Shorts?

If you already have long videos, OpusClip. If you film short clips of yourself, Submagic. For faceless Shorts, Crayo.

### Can AI make a full video from a script?

Yes. VideoGen, InVideo and Fliki all turn scripts into narrated videos with visuals. Expect to swap a few clips and adjust timing.

### Are AI video tools worth paying for?

If you publish several times a week, usually yes, because they save hours of editing. If you post once a month, a free plan or a single month's subscription is often enough.

### Which AI video tool has the best refund policy?

Most have strict policies. Several, including Submagic, VideoGen and Crayo, are non-refundable, so test free plans or trials first.
