## Key takeaways

- **What it is:** an AI clipper that scans a long video, picks the moments worth sharing, and turns them into captioned shorts.
- **What it really costs:** $15 or $29 a month, or about $14.50 a month on yearly Pro. Budget by hours of source video, not by clips.
- **Best for:** podcasters, webinar hosts and YouTubers with a steady supply of long, talk-heavy recordings.
- **Skip it if:** you make short clips already, your content is gaming or fast-cut, or you need every clip to be publishable untouched.
- **Our score:** 8.2/10. A real time-saver, as long as you plan to review what it hands you.

## How we evaluated this

This is a research-based review, without first-hand test footage of our own. We read OpusClip's pricing page, help centre and terms, its Trustpilot profile and first page of one-star reviews, G2 data, and a published 30-day test by ScaleReach. ScaleReach sells a competing tool and says so, so weigh its numbers with that in mind. Everything was checked on November 5, 2026.

## What OpusClip actually is

You give OpusClip a long video, up to 10 hours according to its help centre. It finds segments that could stand alone, reframes them for vertical, adds animated captions, and scores each clip for viral potential. You then edit, schedule or export. The company behind it, Opusclip Inc., is based in Mountain View, California.

The real product here is selection. Adding captions is easy. Watching a 70-minute interview to find the four good minutes is the slow part, and that's the job OpusClip takes off your plate. Note that Agent Opus is a separate product, which matters when you read reviews, because complaints about it land on OpusClip's Trustpilot page too.

## Who it's for

- **The weekly podcaster.** One 60-minute episode a week means 240 credits a month, so you land on Pro. This is the buyer OpusClip was built for.
- **The webinar marketer.** You've got a library of recordings doing nothing. A year of credits up front suits that kind of burst work.
- **The small video team.** Pro exports to Premiere Pro and DaVinci Resolve, so an editor can use it for rough selection and finish elsewhere.

It's a weak fit for someone filming 60-second tips to camera. There's nothing to clip, and you'd be paying for the part you don't need.

## Where it's strong

**It saves real time on talk-heavy video.** In ScaleReach's test, six videos totalling 4 hours 18 minutes produced 76 clips. Of those, 47 were ready to publish as they came, or 62%. Another 19 needed light edits and 10 were unusable. A tool that gives you 47 usable clips from one afternoon of source video has earned its fee, even with a human checking the work.

**Credits are simple, and fair when things break.** One credit is one minute of source video. Partial minutes round down, and a failed job returns its credits automatically, according to the help centre. That's a clearer deal than most credit systems in this category.

**You can start for free.** The free plan gives 60 credits a month, with a watermark. There's also a seven-day Pro trial, and in most countries no card is needed for it.

**G2 users like it.** Comparison pages show about 4.6 out of 5 from roughly 115 reviews, and 9.2 for ease of use.

## What users complain about

OpusClip's Trustpilot score is 4.0 from 515 reviews. It's a split crowd: 63% five-star and 20% one-star, which is a much larger unhappy group than you'd want. Most of those reviews are recent, with 372 from the last 12 months.

We read the first 20 one-star reviews. That's a small slice of about 100, so treat it as a sense of the themes, not a ranking:

1. **Billing: 7.** Mostly charges after cancelling, along with unwanted renewals and refund delays.
2. **Bugs and rendering: 5.** Failed uploads, lost work and lower-quality output.
3. **Support: 5.** Mainly chatbots with no human behind them.
4. **Other: 3.** Includes a project locked after a plan lapsed.

Four of those 20 appear to concern Agent Opus rather than OpusClip, so the numbers overstate things slightly. OpusClip replies to every negative review, often within a day or two, though many of those replies are a template.

**Clip selection divides people.** It didn't show up as a main theme in our sample, yet CheckThat's roundup of public reviews calls inconsistent clip quality the dominant complaint, and users there ask for more control. Both can be true: unhappy payers write about money, while active users grumble about clips.

**The virality score is a hint, not a forecast.** ScaleReach tracked 30 clips for 14 days. Clips scoring above 75 averaged about 2.3 times the views of those below 50, so the score does mean something. But its best performer scored 64 and its worst scored 87. Don't let it make your posting decisions for you.

**Captions need a skim.** OpusClip's own site quotes both 97% and 99%. ScaleReach's 12-clip spot-check found about 95% on clean solo audio and about 88% with overlapping speech or non-American accents. Names and jargon are worth checking before you post.

## Pricing: what you'll really pay

You pay for every minute you upload, whether you keep one clip or thirty. That's the whole model, so budget in hours of source video.

| Who | Monthly source video | Credits | Plan | Monthly billing | Yearly billing |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Interview show | 4 × 30 min | 120 | Starter | $15 | Not offered |
| Weekly podcast | 4 × 60 min | 240 | Pro | $29 | About $14.50 ($174/yr) |
| Twice-weekly long show | 8 × 90 min | 720 | 3 Pro packs | About $87 | About $43.50 |

The last row assumes each extra Pro pack costs the same, and the yearly dollar figures come from third-party pricing guides, which match the 50% discount in OpusClip's help centre. In round numbers, that's about $6 per source hour on monthly plans, and $2.90 on yearly Pro.

Five things worth knowing before you pay:

- **Trim before you process.** A timeframe slider lets you process part of a video. Skipping a 10-minute intro saves 10 credits every episode.
- **Deleting doesn't refund.** Credits on a deleted project are gone. Pro users can refund an undownloaded project within 14 days, so check clips before you download.
- **Monthly credits don't last.** They expire after 60 days, which gives you one month of rollover. Yearly credits arrive up front and last 12 months.
- **You can't pause.** If you take a break, you'd have to cancel and resubscribe.
- **Cancel with care.** The terms say fees already paid aren't refunded, and there's no prorated refund. The help centre also warns that projects go out of reach three days after you cancel, so download what you want first.

**Which plan should you pick?** Add up your source minutes for a typical month. Under 150, take Starter. Between 150 and 300, take Pro. If you publish every week and you're confident you'll stick with it, yearly Pro is the clear value, but do the free plan and trial first.

## OpusClip vs rivals

**Submagic** comes up most often in this search, yet it does a different job. Pick Submagic if your videos are already short and need polish. See our [Submagic vs OpusClip comparison](/compare/submagic-vs-opusclip).

**Vizard and Klap** are the rivals most guides name for clipping. Klap reportedly meters clips instead of minutes, which could suit very long recordings. Their prices vary across the guides we read, so check them directly. Pick one of these if per-minute billing doesn't suit your volume.

**CapCut** is the common free recommendation. Pick CapCut if you'd rather choose moments yourself and don't mind spending the time.

## Your first hour

1. Choose one typical episode, not your best one.
2. Run it on the free plan and note how many credits it takes.
3. Sort the clips into publish, fix and bin. If under half are usable, this may not suit your content.
4. Spot-check captions on a clip with names or jargon.
5. Multiply your credits by your monthly output, then choose a plan.

## Look elsewhere when

- Your content is gaming, or heavy on visuals over talk. ScaleReach cites gaming as a poor fit, and Kompozy says accuracy slips on dense technical lectures.
- You already shoot short clips. Try Submagic.
- You upload many hours a month and don't publish much from them. Per-minute billing punishes that, so compare a clip-metered tool.
- You won't review clips by hand. Roughly four in ten needed work or got binned in the one structured test we found.

## Verdict

OpusClip is the tool we'd start with for turning long, talk-heavy recordings into shorts. It does the tedious part well enough to pay for itself, and its credit rules are fairer than most. It isn't a press-a-button content machine, though. Plan on reviewing every clip, ignore any promise of going viral, and watch your renewal date, because billing is where its reviews turn sour. Use it as a very fast assistant editor and you'll be happy.

## FAQ

### Is OpusClip free?

Yes, there's a free plan with 60 credits a month. Exports carry a watermark and expire after three days.

### How much video can I process each month?

Starter covers 150 minutes of source video and Pro covers 300 per pack. One credit equals one minute.

### Do failed jobs waste credits?

No. OpusClip's help centre says credits return automatically when a project fails. Deleting a finished project doesn't return them.

### Can I get a refund?

The terms say fees already paid aren't refunded. Pro users can refund a single project within 14 days if no clips were downloaded, which returns credits, not money.

### Is the virality score accurate?

It's a rough guide. In one 30-clip test, higher scores did better on average, but the best clip had a middling score.
