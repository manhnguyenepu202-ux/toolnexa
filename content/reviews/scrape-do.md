## What Scrape.do actually is

Scraping at scale runs into the same problems: IP bans, CAPTCHAs, bot protection and pages that only load with JavaScript. Scrape.do sits in the middle. You call its API with a target URL, it routes the request through datacenter, residential or mobile proxies, renders JavaScript if asked, and returns the result.

It also offers ready-made endpoints for some sites, geo-targeting in over 160 countries, sticky sessions and asynchronous jobs. The company behind it is PACKEND, LLC, under US law.

What sets it apart is price on hard targets, plus a promise that failed requests don't cost credits.

## Who it's for

- **The solo developer.** You're building a price tracker or a research dataset and want something that works on protected sites without running your own proxy pool.
- **The small data team.** You scrape a few hundred thousand pages a month and care about cost per successful page more than brand name.
- **The SEO or market-research tool builder.** You need search results and product pages from many countries.

It's a weaker fit for enterprises that need detailed contracts, data processing agreements and named legal entities with addresses. Those things exist at larger vendors, and Scrape.do's public paperwork is thin.

## Where it's strong

**Low cost on protected pages.** A request through residential proxies with JavaScript rendering costs 25 credits. On the $99 Pro plan, 1.25 million credits buy about 50,000 of those pages, or roughly $1.98 per thousand. ZenRows charges the same 25 credits for that type of request, but its $99 plan has 500,000 credits, which works out at about $4.95 per thousand.

**You pay for successes.** The pricing page says timeouts, blocks and errors don't use credits. If you get a 200 response with junk content, it says you can ask support to restore the credits. Each response includes a header showing what that request cost and your remaining balance, which makes budgeting easier.

**Strong results in a public benchmark.** String's October 30, 2026 run sent 100 bot-protected URLs to 17 providers, five times each. Scrape.do passed 94.0% (470 of 500) and placed second, with an average of about 5 seconds. String placed itself first, so it's not neutral. But its test harness is public, and Scrape.do placed well ahead of ScraperAPI (76.4%), Bright Data (75.4%) and ZenRows (41.8%). Scrape.do's own tests claim around 98%, and its homepage claims 99.98%. We'd plan around the independent figure.

**Users like the support.** Trustpilot shows 4.8 from 71 reviews, and the last year of reviews is uniformly five-star. The most common theme is fast, personal support, often naming staff. Capterra shows 5.0 from 51 reviews in search results, though we couldn't open its page to check the detail.

**A real free plan.** 1,000 successful credits a month, 5 concurrent requests, no card. That's enough to test your hardest targets before paying.

## What users complain about

There isn't much negative feedback, and that comes with a caveat. Trustpilot flags that the company hasn't invited reviews recently, many reviews are short, and many reviewers have posted only once. That doesn't make them fake, but it's a small, enthusiastic sample.

**Cancelling wipes your credits.** This is the one specific complaint, and it matters. A June 2025 one-star review says all credits disappeared on cancelling, and a four-star review from August 2025 says the same. Scrape.do's reply to the one-star says the reviewer made an error and was refunded $50. The practical lesson stands either way: use up your credits before you cancel, or cancel close to renewal.

**Site-specific costs can surprise you.** The 1, 5, 10 and 25 credit costs are for ordinary domains. The docs list fixed costs for many popular sites: Google at 10, G2 at 25, and some retail and travel sites at 75, 100 or even 200 credits a request. Check the table for your targets before you estimate.

**Small inconsistencies in the docs.** The pricing page says every feature is available on every plan, but lists premium residential and mobile proxies as a Business feature. One docs page says only 2xx responses use credits, while the request-costs page says 400, 404 and 410 responses are billed too. These are minor, but they're worth asking about if your budget is tight.

**Old legal pages.** The terms and privacy policy are both dated September 26, 2022. They name PACKEND, LLC but give no address. The privacy policy doesn't say how long scraped content is kept. For a hobby project that's fine. For a company with a vendor review, it may not be.

"Protected pages" means residential or mobile proxies plus JavaScript rendering, at 25 credits each. Many sites need less: 5 credits for rendering alone, or 10 for residential proxies alone. Sites with their own fixed cost in the docs can need more.

Yearly billing works as "pay for 10 months, get 12", set up manually through support. That's $290 a year on Hobby and $990 on Pro.

There's no pay-as-you-go overage. When you hit your limit, requests stop until you upgrade or buy extra credits, and we found no published price for extra credits. So size your plan with headroom, or set alerts on the remaining-credits header.

**Refunds are narrow.** The terms allow a refund of unused credits within 3 days of your first subscription. If you forgot to cancel, it's a full refund within 3 days of the renewal charge, or 50% within 15 days. Credits you've used aren't refunded. Use the free plan for testing, not the first paid month.

**Which plan should you pick?** Start on Free with your hardest targets and read the cost header on each response. Most small projects fit Hobby. If more than about a third of your pages need residential proxies with rendering, Pro is the better value. Only consider yearly after a couple of months, because it's manual and the refund window is short.

## Scrape.do vs rivals

**ScraperAPI** is the better known name, with pay-as-you-go on larger plans and a 7-day refund. It costs more per protected page and placed lower in String's benchmark. See our [Scrape.do vs ScraperAPI comparison](/compare/scrape-do-vs-scraperapi).

**ZenRows** has a more generous free plan, at 5,000 credits a month, and fine-grained plan sizes. The same $99 buys far fewer protected pages, and it scored poorly in String's test.

**Bright Data** suits large companies that need contracts, compliance paperwork and a huge proxy network. It costs more and is more complex to set up.

## Your first hour

1. Sign up for the free plan and copy your token.
2. Send three of your real target URLs with default settings.
3. Read the `Scrape.do-Request-Cost` header on each response.
4. Retry any failures with `render=true`, then `super=true`, and note what each needs.
5. Check the domain cost table in the docs for each target site.
6. Multiply cost per page by your monthly volume, and pick a plan with 20% headroom.

## Look elsewhere when

- You need overage billing so jobs never stop mid-month. ScraperAPI offers pay-as-you-go on larger plans.
- Your procurement team needs a full legal entity, address, DPA and current terms. Larger vendors like Bright Data are easier here.
- You mostly scrape simple, unprotected pages. A cheap datacenter proxy or your own scraper may cost less.
- You want to scrape sites whose terms forbid it. No API makes that a good idea, so check the target site's terms and the law where you operate.

## FAQ

### Is Scrape.do free?

Yes, up to 1,000 successful credits a month with 5 concurrent requests, and no card required.

### Does Scrape.do charge for failed requests?

Not for timeouts, blocks or errors, according to its pricing page. Its request-cost docs say 400, 404 and 410 responses are billed.

### What happens to my credits if I cancel?

Users report that cancelling removes remaining credits immediately. Use them first, or cancel near your renewal date.

### Can I get a refund?

Only for unused credits within 3 days of your first subscription. A missed cancellation can get a full refund within 3 days, or 50% within 15.

### Is Scrape.do better than ScraperAPI?

On price per protected page and in String's benchmark, yes. ScraperAPI has overage billing and a longer refund window.
