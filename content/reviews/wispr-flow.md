## What Wispr Flow actually is

You hold a key, speak, and polished text appears wherever your cursor is. It removes filler words, adds punctuation and fixes small slips. It works across email, chat, documents and code editors, on Mac, Windows, iPhone and Android.

The important detail is that the work happens on Wispr's servers. Your audio goes to the cloud, gets transcribed, and comes back as text. That's what makes the text so tidy, and it's the reason for most of the caution below.

## Who it's for

- **The heavy emailer.** You type all day and speak much faster than you type.
- **The founder or manager.** You want quick replies, across phone and laptop.
- **The developer.** You dictate prompts to AI coding tools.
- **Someone with wrist pain.** Dictation can take real strain off your hands.

It's a poor fit for lawyers, doctors and anyone handling sensitive data on the individual plans. It's also useless on a plane or a bad connection.

## Where it's strong

**Clean text straight away.** Reviewers consistently praise how little editing the output needs. That's the difference between dictation you try once and dictation you keep using.

**It works everywhere.** There's no special editor to paste from. It types into the app you're already using.

**Cross-platform.** Mac, Windows, iOS and Android are all covered, and that's wider than many rivals.

**Clear data controls, now.** Wispr's current data page is plain about each setting, and it says audio is deleted after transcription unless you turn on cloud storage. It also claims SOC 2 Type II and ISO 27001.

**Students get 50% off.** That applies to anyone with an education email.

## What users complain about

We have less direct user evidence here than usual. Trustpilot's page for wisprflow.ai now says the profile has been removed because the business goes against its guidelines, and it doesn't give details. We don't know why. An older regional listing showed about 2.5, and a competitor's article cited about 2.7, with 4.5 on G2. Treat those as old and second-hand.

The recurring themes across reviews are:

1. **Privacy.** By far the biggest topic.
2. **No offline mode.** You can't dictate without a connection.
3. **Subscription cost.** Especially against tools with one-off pricing.
4. **Performance.** Some reviews mention the app feeling heavy, though we couldn't measure how common that is.

**The 2025 privacy row.** Several blogs describe a Reddit user who monitored network traffic, and reported Wispr Flow uploading screenshots of the active window. Those accounts say the user was first banned, and that the company's CTO then apologised and promised clearer controls. Most of these write-ups come from competing dictation apps, so read them with care. Wispr's current documents say context awareness reads limited text from the app you're using, and that it can be turned off. Some rivals still dispute how it works. We can't verify that either way.

**A small conflict in the fine print.** The data controls page says Wispr keeps zero data retention agreements with AI providers. The terms say shared data with OpenAI or Anthropic is deleted after 30 days. These may cover different cases, but it's a question to ask if you're buying for a company.

## The settings to change on day one

Do this before you start dictating anything real:

1. **Turn off "Improve the model for everyone".** With it off, Wispr says nothing you say is used for training. We couldn't confirm the default, so check it.
2. **Turn off Dictation cloud storage.** Audio is then discarded after transcription.
3. **Consider turning off Context awareness.** Dictation still works, without reading your screen.
4. **Set local history to auto-delete, or to never save.**

One catch: if you rate a transcription or send feedback, that session can be used for training, even with the setting off. So avoid doing that on sensitive text.

On Growth and Enterprise, training is off by default and locked, and that's the better fit for teams.

Wispr's page doesn't give the free limit. Third-party reviews put it at 2,000 words a week on desktop and 1,000 on iPhone. At a normal speaking pace, 2,000 words is about 13 minutes of talking, so a daily user will hit it by midweek.

Is Pro worth it? If dictation saves you ten minutes a day, then $12 a month is easy to justify. If you dictate a few messages a week, stay on Free.

Refunds are only issued if the law requires them, so don't start on yearly. Use Free for a week, then Pro monthly for a month.

**Which plan should you pick?** Most individuals only need Pro. Choose Growth for a company, because locked training settings are worth the extra $8 a month.

## Verdict

Wispr Flow is one of the most pleasant ways to write without typing, and for everyday emails and chat it's easy to recommend. The trade-off is that your voice leaves your device every time, with a company that has already had to apologise once over privacy. If you're comfortable with that, change the four settings and pay monthly. If you're not, use a local tool instead. Don't dictate anything you wouldn't put in an email to a stranger until you've checked your settings.

## FAQ

### Is Wispr Flow free?

Yes, with a weekly word limit. Third-party reviews put it at 2,000 words on desktop.

### Does Wispr Flow work offline?

No. Wispr says transcription always happens in the cloud.

### Does Wispr Flow record my screen?

Its current documents say context awareness reads limited text from the app you're dictating into, and you can turn it off. Older versions drew criticism for screenshots.

### Does Wispr Flow train on my voice?

Only if the setting is on. Turn off "Improve the model for everyone", and avoid sending feedback on sensitive text.

### Can I get a refund?

Only if the law requires it, according to the terms.
