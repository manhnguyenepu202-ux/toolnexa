## What BounceBan actually is

A catch-all domain accepts mail for any address, so a normal verifier can't tell whether jane@company.com really exists. Most tools shrug and label it risky. If you sell to businesses, that shrug can cover a big slice of your list.

BounceBan exists for that slice. It uses its own mix of signals to call those addresses deliverable or not, without sending them an email, and it covers Google Workspace, Office 365 and gateways like Proofpoint and Mimecast. Each address gets a state (deliverable, risky, undeliverable or unknown) plus a 0 to 100 score.

It does one thing. There's no warm-up, no inbox placement testing and no reputation monitoring.

## Who it's for

- **The founder doing outbound.** You've bought or scraped 8,000 leads and half the domains are catch-all. A $40 pack costs less than one burned domain.
- **The SDR team.** You're enriching in Clay and want a verdict before a sequence fires. BounceBan connects to Clay, n8n and Google Sheets, or you can use the API.
- **The outbound agency.** Volume pricing and rollover credits suit uneven client work.

It's a weaker fit for a newsletter or an e-commerce list. Consumer mailboxes are where its benchmark results dip, as we'll see.

## Where it's strong

**Almost everything gets a verdict.** In Allegrow's test of 10,000 records across 251 catch-all domains, BounceBan left one record unresolved. Compare that with NeverBounce, which left 9,633 unresolved in the same test. If you've been throwing away every catch-all address, this is the difference between a list and half a list.

**Few false positives there.** It marked 9 of 5,000 invented addresses as valid, or 0.18%. It found 460 of 500 real people. That placed it third of 11 tools, behind Allegrow at 491 and ZeroBounce at up to 470.

**Business domains look solid.** Even in ZeroBounce's own benchmark, BounceBan scored 96.66% on business domains, including 99.59% on Google Workspace and 94.20% on Proofpoint.

**Free checks are generous.** Single lookups in Free Mode have no monthly limit, subject to fair use, so you can test addresses you already know the answer to before paying a cent.

**Credits don't expire.** They roll over, which is rarer than it should be in this market.

## What users complain about

BounceBan has a strong G2 record, about 4.8 out of 5 from roughly 300 reviews, though we could only see that second-hand. We found no Trustpilot or Capterra profile, so there's less public complaint data than for bigger names. A small sample of critical G2 comments, reported by search snippets and by Allegrow, a rival, centres on these:

1. **Cost and credits.** Some feel pricing is high for small lists, or that free credits are too few for serious testing.
2. **Paying for bad addresses.** Allegrow reports that undeliverable and risky results cost the same credit as good ones.
3. **Speed on big lists.** BounceBan itself says some addresses take one to six minutes.
4. **Integrations and dashboard.** Users want more of both, plus more detail on why addresses failed.

The bigger question is the benchmark gap, so here it is plainly.

**The accuracy numbers disagree, and both tests have an interest.** ZeroBounce's July 2026 test put BounceBan at 86.06% accurate against its own 98.02%, with 13.57% wrong. Allegrow's test placed BounceBan just behind ZeroBounce. The biggest drag in ZeroBounce's test was consumer mail: Yahoo came in at 55.25%, and free domains overall at 82.65%. That fits what the product is for, but it's a real warning if your list has plenty of personal addresses.

**Each benchmark has soft spots.** ZeroBounce's ground truth relied partly on its own engineers settling disputes. Allegrow's "real people" were matched from public LinkedIn data, and it admits their mailboxes weren't confirmed. So neither proves the 97%+ claim, and neither disproves it. The only benchmark that matters is one run on your list.

**The claims shift from page to page.** BounceBan says 83%+ of catch-alls on one help page, 85 to 95% on its guarantee page, and over 90% on its pricing page. Treat these as a range.

**Valid doesn't mean the person is still there.** Allegrow notes that BounceBan's own DeepVerify warning says results can be wrong if the owner has left the company. No verifier replaces fresh data.

## Legal and security checks

This section won't matter to a solo founder. It'll matter a lot to anyone with a procurement team.

- Terms and privacy notice were last updated in November 2022.
- The privacy notice still cites the EU-U.S. Privacy Shield, which was struck down in 2020.
- We found a Dover, Delaware mailing address, but no legal entity name on the pages we read.
- The privacy notice describes building profiles of business people from public sources and sharing some with partners. Read that before uploading client data.
- Allegrow says no SOC 2 audit was stated on the site as of October 2026.

BounceBan says it follows GDPR and CCPA. If that matters to you, ask for a data processing agreement before uploading anything. None of this means something is wrong, but paperwork this dated is a fair reason to ask.

## Pricing: what you'll really pay

There are no tiers to decode. You buy credits, either pay-as-you-go or on a monthly subscription at about 15% off. One credit covers one verification.

| Who | Monthly volume | Pay-as-you-go | Subscription | Per 1,000 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Founder, occasional list | 10,000 | $40 | $34 | $3.40 to $4.00 |
| Small SDR team | 50,000 | $175 | $149 | $2.98 to $3.50 |
| Outbound agency | 100,000 | $300 | $255 | $2.55 to $3.00 |
| High volume | 1,000,000 | $1,700 | $1,450 | $1.45 to $1.70 |

Since credits don't expire, pay-as-you-go makes sense for irregular work. Subscribe only if you'll use the credits every month.

Four things to watch:

- **Purchases are non-refundable.** The terms say so, and that sits awkwardly beside the guarantee.
- **The guarantee is narrow.** It only covers addresses marked deliverable that hard bounce, sent within 24 hours of verifying, on a list of at least 1,000 emails across 100 domains, claimed within seven days. Allegrow says the remedy is credits, not cash.
- **Don't resubmit while checking.** The API docs warn that every submit costs a credit, so a sloppy integration can double your bill. Poll for the result instead.
- **Charges on unknowns are unclear.** The docs say "successful" verifications cost a credit without defining it. Allegrow says bulk jobs refund unknown results. Check this in your account history.

Sync, which cleans your CRM daily, is a separate subscription.

**Which option should you pick?** Buy one 10,000 pack first. Only subscribe after a real send proves it on your list.

## Your first hour

1. Run 20 addresses you know through Free Mode: live colleagues, and people who've left.
2. Buy the 10,000 pack and verify a sample of your real list.
3. Send to at least 500 deliverable addresses, as BounceBan's help centre suggests.
4. Measure hard bounces. Its help centre says anything above 3% needs a closer look.
5. Decide what to do with risky results before they reach a sequence.

## Look elsewhere when

- Your list is mainly Yahoo, Gmail and other personal addresses. ZeroBounce looks stronger here.
- Procurement needs SOC 2, or current legal documents.
- You want warm-up and blacklist monitoring in the same login.
- You're verifying a list of mostly non-catch-all domains. Cheaper basic tools will do.

## Verdict

For B2B outbound lists, BounceBan is the verifier we'd try first. It's cheap enough to test on a real list, it gives you a verdict where most tools won't, and credits you don't use stick around. Go in with your eyes open, though. The accuracy claims aren't independently proven, consumer addresses look like a weak spot, and the legal pages need updating. And remember that verification keeps your bounce rate down. It doesn't make cold email welcome or compliant.

## FAQ

### Is BounceBan free?

Single checks in Free Mode are free with no monthly limit, subject to fair use. Bulk, API and Premium checks cost one credit each.

### How much does BounceBan cost?

$40 for 10,000 credits pay-as-you-go, or $34 on a monthly subscription. The price falls to $1,700 or $1,450 for a million.

### Do BounceBan credits expire?

No. BounceBan says unused credits roll over and don't expire.

### Is BounceBan accurate?

BounceBan claims 97%+ on catch-alls. Two rival-run benchmarks disagree: one placed it third of 11, and another scored it 86.06% overall. Test it on your own list.

### Does BounceBan send emails to verify addresses?

No. It says it verifies without sending messages, using its own signals, including SMTP testing.
