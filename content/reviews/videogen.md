## What VideoGen actually is

VideoGen started as a script-to-video tool and has grown into something broader. You can begin with a script, a voiceover, a slide deck, a storyboard or an existing video, and it assembles visuals, narration and captions. There's an editor for changes, an API for developers, and Zapier for automation. The company, VideoGen, Inc., says it was founded in 2022 and lists a San Francisco address.

The appeal is that one subscription reaches more than 20 AI models. You don't need separate accounts for video, image and voice, because everything draws on one credit balance. That's also where the trouble starts, as we'll see.

## Who it's for

- **The content marketer.** You turn blog posts into narrated explainers each week. Still-image video uses few credits, so Pro goes a long way.
- **The course creator.** You've got decks already, and slides-to-video with AI narration saves recording time.
- **The developer.** There's an API, an MCP server and SDKs, although commercial production use appears to need an add-on The Rundown listed at $360 a month in August.

It's a poor fit for anyone who needs a person on the phone if a charge goes wrong, or for a team that wants cinematic footage on a fixed budget.

## Where it's strong

**Lots of ways in.** Few rivals accept as many starting points. If your team already has decks, scripts and voiceovers, you can use them instead of starting from a prompt.

**Clear published rates.** The rate card lists what each action costs, which is more than most rivals share. It's easy to read, even if the totals sting.

**Commercial rights on all plans.** VideoGen's pricing page says every plan includes full commercial use.

**Bought credits last.** Credits you purchase separately never expire, and 1080p exports cost nothing extra. Plan credits are different: they reset monthly.

## What users complain about

VideoGen's Trustpilot score is 2.7 from 108 reviews, with 55% five-star and 35% one-star. That split matters. Just 12 reviews arrived in the last 12 months, so the older positive reviews are holding up the average. Half of the latest 20 one-star reviews were posted between January and July 2026.

Here's what those 20 reviews describe. Reviews can touch several themes, so the counts overlap:

1. **Billing, cancelling and refunds: about 12.** Reviewers report charges after cancelling, trouble finding how to cancel, and refused refunds. Some call it a scam. That's their word, not a finding of ours, but this many similar reports deserve attention.
2. **Support: about 9.** Chatbot loops, scripted replies and emails left unanswered. Trustpilot's profile badge says the company hasn't replied to negative reviews, and we saw only one reply in that sample.
3. **Product quality and bugs: about 7.** Repetitive or irrelevant output, failed downloads and rejected uploads.
4. **Confusing credits: about 3.** Some reviewers couldn't tell where their credits went.

Other sources lean the same way, but they're thin. Capterra showed 1.2 from just five reviews, in the search summaries we could see. Product Hunt shows 3.3 from four older reviews, including praise for speed and complaints about bugs and limited editing. Cybernews reportedly found the voiceover sounded AI-generated, and The Rundown warns that a fast first cut still needs human editing.

There's a fair counterpoint: we found very few reviews overall for a company claiming over 5 million users, and unhappy customers write more often. Some reviewers also report quick refunds. But the pattern is consistent, and it's recent.

## Pricing: what you'll really pay

Pro's headline is "~4 min of generative video". That estimate assumes Standard quality and one version of each video, with no revisions. Quality changes everything:

| Quality | AI video credits per second | What Pro's 6,000 credits buy |
|---|---|---|
| Low | 10 | About 10 minutes |
| Standard | 20 | About 5 minutes |
| High | 100 | 60 seconds |
| Max | 200 | 30 seconds (needs Advanced or Ultra) |

These figures cover generation only, and workflow fees come on top. So on Pro, High quality gives you one minute of footage a month. Advanced's 18,000 credits buy 90 seconds at Max.

Real scenarios look like this:

| Who | Monthly work | Plan | Monthly billing | Yearly billing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Blog-to-video marketer | 4 five-minute still-image videos | Pro | $29 | $19 |
| Ad creative, one take | 4 × 30 s at Standard, 2,400 credits | Pro | $29 | $19 |
| Ad creative, three takes | 7,200 credits | Pro + $12 top-up | $41 | $31 |
| Training team | About 60 min of avatar video | Ultra | $149 | $99 |

Extra credits cost $1 per 100, or $10 per 1,000. Plan credits work out far cheaper, between $1.65 and $4.83 per 1,000. One minute of top-up footage costs $12 at Standard, $60 at High and $120 at Max.

Four things to watch:

- **Plan credits don't roll over.** They reset each month, so a quiet month is money lost.
- **Automatic top-ups can raise your bill.** Check that setting before you start.
- **Exporting needs a paid plan.** You can try it free, but the help centre says video export requires payment.
- **Rates can move.** VideoGen raised motion graphics from 30 to 100 credits in October 2026. Its API page still says "$16", while the pricing page says $19, so check at checkout.

**Which plan should you pick?** Pro on monthly billing. The terms say all purchases are non-refundable, and a yearly plan means paying $228 or more up front to a company with this complaint record. The 33% saving isn't worth that risk until you've seen several renewals go smoothly.

## Your first hour

1. Use the free tier on a real script before paying anything.
2. Note the credits used for one video, then multiply by your monthly output.
3. Find the auto top-up setting and switch it off if you want a hard budget.
4. If you subscribe, choose monthly and find the cancel option straight away.
5. Set a reminder a week ahead of renewal, and keep proof when you cancel.

## Look elsewhere when

- You want dependable billing support. Try Synthesia.
- You need top-quality generative clips in volume. Compare Runway's costs.
- You're on a tight budget and want a guaranteed monthly bill.
- You need to verify rights to every asset. VideoGen's terms date from 2024, so have someone read them.

## Verdict

VideoGen's product is better than its reputation, and its reputation is still the deciding factor. For narrated, still-image videos, Pro on monthly billing is decent value, and the breadth of inputs is useful. But we wouldn't buy a yearly plan, and we wouldn't leave automatic top-ups running. Too many recent reviewers describe the same trouble getting out and getting answers. Try it on one real project, keep it monthly, and be ready to cancel early.

## FAQ

### Is VideoGen free?

You can start for free, but the help centre says video export requires a paid plan. Paid plans start at $19 per user a month billed yearly, or $29 monthly.

### Can I get a refund from VideoGen?

The terms say all purchases are non-refundable. Some users report refunds anyway, and others report refusals, so don't count on one.

### How do I cancel VideoGen?

The terms say you can cancel by logging in or contacting the company, effective at the end of the paid term. We couldn't find a help article with the exact steps, so find the option before you need it.

### Do VideoGen credits roll over?

Plan credits reset monthly. Extra credits you buy separately never expire.

### Is VideoGen a scam?

We have no evidence of that. It's a real, funded product, but it does have a poor billing and support record among reviewers.
