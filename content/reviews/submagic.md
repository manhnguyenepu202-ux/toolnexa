## Key takeaways

- **What it is:** an AI editor that turns short, talking-head videos into captioned, punched-up clips ready for TikTok, Reels and Shorts.
- **What it really costs:** $19, $39 or $69 a month, or $12, $23 or $41 on annual billing, per member. Long-video clipping may cost extra.
- **Best for:** creators and small teams who record to camera and want polished captions in minutes.

## How we evaluated this

This is a research-based review, without first-hand test footage of our own. We read Submagic's pricing page, help centre and terms, then 29 of its lowest-rated Trustpilot reviews in full, plus G2 data and independent write-ups. Everything was checked on November 5, 2026.

## What Submagic actually is

Submagic is a finisher for short videos, not a full video editor. You upload a clip, it transcribes the speech, and you get animated captions in a template. From there you cut by editing the transcript rather than dragging a timeline. It can also remove silences, add B-roll, zoom, drop in sound effects and translate captions. The company behind it is Turbo Studio, a French firm based in Paris.

What sets it apart is focus. Plenty of tools add captions. Submagic built its whole product around making a talking-head clip look like it came from a professional shorts editor, and the praise in user reviews follows that same line: fast, easy, good-looking output.

## Who it's for

- **The solo creator posting three times a week.** You film 60-second tips on your phone, and you're tired of placing captions by hand. This is the ideal buyer, and Starter covers you.
- **The coach or founder doing LinkedIn clips.** Three-minute talks need Pro, because Starter stops at two minutes per video.
- **The small agency finishing client shorts.** Business gets you longer videos, 4K export and priority rendering, though the per-member maths matters here.

It's a poor fit for a podcaster who wants a single upload turned into a month of clips. That job belongs to a dedicated clipper, and we'll get to why.

## Where it's strong

**Speed to a finished clip.** Ease of use is the most consistent praise across G2 and Trustpilot. Reviewers keep describing the same thing: upload, pick a style, export, done. G2 comparison pages show 9.3 for ease of use, although those figures may be dated.

**Captions on clean speech.** Not one of the 29 low-star Trustpilot reviews we read complained about caption accuracy, and one even praised it while panning Magic Clips and exports. That's a strong signal, because angry reviewers rarely hold back.

**No watermark on any paid plan.** Even the $19 Starter exports clean, at 1080p.

**Transcript editing.** Cutting words instead of waveforms suits people who aren't editors. The trade is less precision than a timeline gives you.

## What users complain about

Submagic's Trustpilot score is 4.5 from 859 reviews, with 87% five-star and 7% one-star. Those are good numbers, though not the 4.8 its homepage displays. G2 snapshots we found sit at 4.6 to 4.7 from about 85 reviews, also below the 4.9 advertised. Trustpilot notes that the company hasn't invited reviews recently, and every review we saw was unprompted, which counts in its favour.

Among the 29 low-star reviews we read (the first 20 of roughly 60 one-star reviews, plus nine two-star), the themes ranked like this:

1. **Bugs, rendering and export problems: 8.** Crashes, audio drifting out of sync, and exports that came out lower quality than the preview. Recent cases run through August 2026, so this isn't ancient history.
2. **Billing and renewals: 7.** Mostly surprise renewals, plus people who say they were charged after cancelling.
3. **Refunds refused or delayed: 7.** This one is policy, not a glitch. The terms say fees are non-refundable.
4. **Support:** only two reviews made it the main issue, but about 17 mentioned it somewhere.

A small sample of unhappy customers can't tell you how often things go wrong. It does tell you what goes wrong when they do, and money comes up more than captions.

One claim we couldn't back up: a reseller blog says failed jobs burn credits with no refund. No low-star review we read described this, and the help centre doesn't address it. We'd treat it as possible, not proven.

The 99% accuracy claim deserves salt too. Two third-party write-ups put it nearer 94 to 96% on clean English and about 88 to 92% with accents or noise, with weaker results for some non-European languages. Neither publishes a method, so these are rough guides. The language count also varies: the homepage says 123, while localised pages say 50+.

## Pricing: what you'll really pay

Everything runs on credits. Uploading a video costs 3, captions are free, each extra AI feature costs 1 to 3, and Magic Clips cost 8. The "15 videos" on Starter only holds if you stick to captions.

| Who | Monthly use | Credits needed | Plan | Monthly billing | Annual billing |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Solo creator, captions only | 12 videos | 36 | Starter | $19 | $12 ($144/yr) |
| Same creator, two AI extras each | 12 videos | 60 to 108 | Pro | $39 | $23 ($276/yr) |
| Three-person team | Up to 100 videos each | Up to 300 each | Business | $207 | $123 ($1,476/yr) |

Run those numbers once before you buy. Add two AI extras per video and Starter's 45 credits cover only five to nine videos, so the plan you actually need may be one tier higher than the headline suggests. The cost per video at full use ranges from $1.27 on Starter to $0.69 on Business.

Three traps to know about:

- **Length caps ignore your credit balance.** Starter stops at 2 minutes per video, Pro at 5, Business at 30. A six-minute clip needs Business, however few videos you make.
- **Magic Clips pricing is murky.** One help page says 8 credits each, another calls it an add-on subscription, and it isn't listed on the plan cards. Competitor and reseller sites quote $19 a month, or $12 on annual billing, per member. Confirm with support before you budget for it.
- **The pricing page disagrees with itself.** Plan cards and the comparison table differ on API minutes and custom templates, so check the feature you care about inside the app.

We couldn't confirm whether unused credits roll over. Top-ups exist, but no price is published in the help article we read.

**Which plan should you pick?** Start on monthly Starter even though it's the worst value per video. Only move to annual after a full month of real use, because that discount comes with a year you can't get back.

## Submagic vs rivals

**OpusClip** is the main alternative. It starts at $15 a month, has a free plan, and is built around clipping long recordings. Pick OpusClip if you start from podcasts or webinars. See our [Submagic vs OpusClip comparison](/compare/submagic-vs-opusclip).

**CapCut** comes up constantly as the free option. You'll spend longer per video, but you get a real timeline. Pick CapCut if you have more time than budget and want full control.

**Captions**, from the company now called Mirage, targets talking-head polish in a mobile-first app, though its plans changed during 2026, so check current tiers. Pick Captions if you film and edit entirely on your phone.

## Your first hour

1. Use the trial on your real content. It covers three short videos, and a paid plan is needed for clean downloads.
2. Pick your hardest clip, the one with an accent, background noise or jargon, and read every caption.
3. Note how many credits one finished video really consumed at `app.submagic.co/user?tab=usage`.
4. Multiply that by your monthly output before you pick a plan.
5. Set a calendar reminder a week ahead of any renewal date.

## Look elsewhere when

- Your source videos run past 30 minutes. Try OpusClip.
- You need frame-accurate editing. Try CapCut.
- You want a generous free plan. Submagic has a trial, not a free tier.
- Your audience needs right-to-left or less common languages and you can't test them first. Claims here vary, so verify before paying.
- A surprise renewal would genuinely hurt. Stay monthly, or choose a vendor with a refund window.

## Verdict

Submagic is one of the quickest ways to make a talking-head clip look properly edited, and for that job it's easy to recommend. We'd buy it monthly, for captions, with eyes open about credits. We wouldn't pay for a year up front until it had survived a month of your real videos, and we wouldn't buy it to chop up long podcasts. The product is better than its billing experience, which is the most honest summary we can give.

## FAQ

### Is Submagic free?

No permanent free plan is listed. You get a trial of three short videos, and a paid plan is needed to download without a watermark.

### Does Submagic offer refunds?

Its terms say monthly and annual fees are non-refundable. EU consumers have a 14-day withdrawal right with exceptions. Some users report refunds granted anyway, but don't count on it.

### How many videos do you really get?

Divide your credits by what each video uses. Captions only costs 3 credits, so Starter gives about 15. Each extra AI feature adds 1 to 3 credits, so the number falls quickly.

### Can Submagic handle long videos?

Only up to 30 minutes per video, and only on Business. Starter stops at 2 minutes and Pro at 5.

### Is Submagic better than OpusClip?

For polishing short talking-head clips, we'd lean Submagic. For turning long recordings into many clips, OpusClip is the better fit.
