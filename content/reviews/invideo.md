## What InVideo actually is

InVideo began as a template editor and now calls itself an agentic video editor. You upload footage or describe what you want, and an agent writes, generates and edits, while you review the cut on a multitrack timeline. It connects to dozens of models, including Veo, Kling and Seedance, so you aren't tied to one.

There are two things to keep straight. Timeline edits and exports are free. Generation, transcripts and paid stock cost credits, and the agent spends those credits for you. That split explains nearly every complaint below.

## Who it's for

- **The short-form creator.** You want 30-second social videos quickly, and you don't mind reviewing drafts.
- **The small marketing team.** Plus includes guest seats, and live collaboration suits quick rounds of feedback.
- **The editor who wants an assistant.** You can bring your own footage and use the agent for rough cuts, which is often cheaper than generating everything.

It's a poor fit for anyone making long explainers on a fixed budget. Reviewers' tests suggest quality drops off past about 90 seconds, and longer videos cost proportionally more.

## Where it's strong

**The agent idea is ahead of many rivals.** Telling software to find a moment in your footage or apply one instruction across a timeline is a real time-saver.

**Speed gets praise.** Small independent reviews consistently rate speed highly, even when they mark down the output.

**Edits don't cost credits.** Timeline changes are free, including changes made by outside agents. That rewards doing the fine work by hand.

**Good aggregate ratings.** G2 search summaries show about 4.3 from 178 reviews, and Capterra about 4.6 from 408, with ease of use praised on both. Those totals may include years of the older product, so we give the recent Trustpilot reviews more weight.

**Short refund window.** The terms offer a refund within seven days of purchase or renewal, but only for unused plans. The docs define used as anything generated or downloaded, and call refunds discretionary.

## What users complain about

InVideo has two Trustpilot profiles, which confuses things. The current AI product shows 2.9 from 910 reviews, with 60% five-star, 30% one-star, and 344 reviews in the last 12 months. The older Invideo Studio profile shows 1.8 from 1,028.

We read the 20 latest one-star reviews on the AI profile, all dated between September 18 and November 3, 2026. Themes overlap:

1. **Credits vanish: about 15.** Reviewers describe a month's credits gone in minutes or on one short video. The same theme led the Studio profile too, at 11 of 20.
2. **Output needs rework: about 9.** Errors and redoing work, which costs more credits.
3. **Refunds and support: about 6.** Refused refunds and unanswered tickets. InVideo replied to 19% of negative reviews.
4. **Crashes and slow renders: about 4.**
5. **Surprise charges: about 3.** One reviewer says a $150 top-up was on by default, and another reports $411. We couldn't confirm either.

There's a likely cause for much of this. InVideo's docs say the agent's default permission is "Always allow", so it generates without asking. Stricter options exist, but you have to turn them on.

Trustpilot's own summary also mentions praise for support and creative potential, so experiences clearly vary. Still, a pattern this recent and this specific is worth planning around.

## Pricing: what you'll really pay

| Plan | Monthly | Yearly, per month | Credits | Per 1,000 credits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Starter | $20 | $10 | 400 | $25 to $50 |
| Plus | $60 | $30 | 2,000 | $15 to $30 |
| Max | $250 | $250 | 8,000 | $31.25 |

Prices are per seat. Plan credits reset each month, and extra credits expire too, unlike at some rivals.

Here's what Plus's 2,000 credits buy using InVideo's published Seedance 2.5 rates:

| Quality | Credits per 5 seconds | Footage from 2,000 credits |
|---|---|---|
| Draft | 24.5 | About 6.8 minutes |
| 720p | 56 | About 3 minutes |
| 1080p | 96 | About 1 minute 44 seconds |

That's one take of each second, with no retries. Four 30-second ads at 1080p need about 2,304 credits, which already exceeds Plus. The same four as drafts need about 588.

Some things to know before you pay:

- **Starter is tight.** It doesn't include Seedance 2.5 at all, and 400 credits go quickly.
- **Max looks like poor value.** There's no yearly discount, so credits cost about twice the yearly Plus rate. Compare several Plus seats first, and check how credits work across seats.
- **Draft first.** Sort out the idea at Draft quality, then spend on the final version.
- **Check for failed runs.** We found no statement that credits return for a failed or unusable generation.

**Which plan should you pick?** Plus on monthly billing for your first month, so you learn your real credit use. Yearly saves 50%, but the refund rule makes that a commitment.

## Your first hour

1. Before anything else, change the agent's permission to "Ask before generating videos".
2. Check your top-up settings, so you know nothing buys credits automatically.
3. Make one 30-second video at Draft quality and note the credits used.
4. Do the fine edits by hand on the timeline, because those are free.
5. Find the cancel option under Subscriptions and set a renewal reminder.

## Look elsewhere when

- You make videos over about 90 seconds regularly. Compare VideoGen on credit cost.
- Your videos need a consistent presenter. Try Synthesia.
- You need polished long-form editing. A traditional editor still does this better.
- You can't afford surprises on your card. Stick to a tool with a hard monthly limit.

## Verdict

InVideo's agent is genuinely clever, and for short videos with a watchful human it can be very quick. The problem is that the default setup lets it spend freely, and many people only learn the cost after their credits have gone. Switch on approvals, draft cheaply, and treat your credits like cash. Do that and it's a useful tool. Skip those steps, and you could easily end up in those reviews.

## FAQ

### Is InVideo free?

Its terms mention a free tier, with limits shown at sign-up, but the pricing page lists only paid plans. We couldn't confirm what the free tier includes.

### Why do InVideo credits run out so fast?

Generation is priced per second by model and quality, and the agent generates without asking by default. Turn on approvals and use Draft quality to slow it down.

### Do InVideo credits roll over?

No. Plan credits reset monthly, and extra credits expire after a set period or when your plan ends.

### Can I get a refund from InVideo?

Only within seven days and only if the plan is unused, which means nothing generated or downloaded. The docs say refunds are discretionary.

### Is InVideo better than VideoGen?

For agent-led editing, it's more ambitious. For cost per video at entry level, we lean VideoGen.
