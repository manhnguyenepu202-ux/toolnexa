Every AI tool now sells itself with a free plan, a trial or a "cancel anytime" badge. Fewer tell you what happens when you've paid and it doesn't work for you. So we went back through the terms, refund policies and help centres we read for our reviews, and lined them up side by side.

The short version: if you pay for an AI or SaaS tool and change your mind, the default answer is no. Half of the 26 tools we checked don't offer refunds in their written terms at all. Most of the rest only refund if you've barely used the product, which means a refund window is rarely a trial.

## Key findings

- **13 of 26 tools (50%) refuse refunds in their written terms**, apart from what the law requires.
- **Another 6 (23%) allow only a narrow exception**, from 24 hours to 14 days, and usually only if you haven't used credits, generated anything or downloaded results.
- **7 tools (27%) offer 14 days or more, and every one of them comes with a condition.** None of the 26 offers a plain, no-questions 30-day refund.
- **Refunds are a top complaint where policies are strict.** In the recent one-star reviews we sampled, refund refusals were counted 16 times for Domestika and 13 times for Base44, together with billing.
- **Yearly discounts make this sharper.** Several tools pair a 30% to 67% saving for paying a year up front with terms that won't refund it.

## How we did this

We used the 26 tools we reviewed or compared between November 5 and November 10, 2026. For each, we read the refund policy, terms of service, pricing FAQ or help centre, and classified the tool by the most generous rule it puts in writing. One data point, for Skillshare, comes from its public replies to reviewers, because its own pages didn't load for us. Each tool links to the review where we documented the source.

There are limits worth stating plainly. This is the set of tools we happened to review, not a random sample of the market, and it leans towards AI video, writing and creator tools. Policies change, so a rule we read in November may not apply when you buy. And we didn't request refunds ourselves: this is what the documents say, not a test of what support does. Where users report a different experience, we say so.

What stands out isn't that these companies refuse refunds. Plenty of software works that way. It's how often the "no" sits next to marketing that suggests otherwise. BounceBan publishes a guarantee while its terms say purchases are non-refundable. Fliki's FAQ and terms don't say quite the same thing. When the documents disagree, the stricter one is the one that tends to get applied.

### 2. A narrow exception (6 tools)

| Tool | The window | The condition |
|---|---|---|
| Tripo | 24 hours | A mistaken purchase, with no credits used |
| Skillshare | About 48 hours after a trial converts | Based on its replies to reviewers, not a written policy |
| getimg.ai | 3 days from purchase or renewal | Fewer than 100 credits used; promotions excluded |
| Toolsaday | 3 days from purchase | For dissatisfaction; nothing after a renewal |
| InVideo | 7 days from purchase or renewal | Plan unused, meaning nothing generated or downloaded |
| OpusClip | 14 days | Pro projects you haven't downloaded only |

These look friendlier than group one, and for accidental purchases they are. But read the conditions. On InVideo, "used" means anything generated or downloaded. On getimg.ai, 100 credits buys a handful of premium images. The window protects you from a wrong click, not from discovering after real use that the tool isn't good enough.

### 3. Fourteen days, with conditions (3 tools)

| Tool | The rule | The catch |
|---|---|---|
| AhaSlides | Full refund within 14 days | Only if you haven't used it at a live event; its terms are stricter than its FAQ |
| Mentimeter | Full refund within 14 days on Basic and Pro | Its FAQ adds the same live-event condition; renewals aren't covered |
| Domestika | 14 days on individual course purchases | Doesn't apply to the Plus subscription |

The live-event condition at AhaSlides and Mentimeter is the cleverest wording in the set. It's fair to the vendor, because you can't run your conference and then ask for your money back. It also means the refund only protects you if you test before the event that matters, which most people don't.

### 4. Thirty days, with conditions (4 tools)

| Tool | The rule | The catch |
|---|---|---|
| EaseUS Data Recovery | 30 days | Technical failures only, after support has tried, and not if a recovery succeeded |
| Kommodo | 30 days | First paid subscription only, once per customer |
| Superwhisper | 30 days | First purchase only, not renewals |
| Slido | 30-day money-back guarantee on its pricing page | Its FAQ says each case is evaluated individually |

These are the best terms in the set, and they're still not unconditional. Kommodo and Superwhisper are the cleanest: if your first purchase doesn't work out, you have a month. EaseUS's policy reads generously on the sales page, but the conditions are tight, and refund disputes are the most repeated complaint in its user reviews.

## Where strict policies meet unhappy customers

A strict policy isn't automatically a problem. If the product works, nobody asks for a refund. The trouble starts when a strict policy meets billing surprises, like a renewal the customer didn't expect or credits that ran out faster than planned.

In the recent one-star reviews we read for our reviews, usually the latest 20 per tool, this is how often refunds came up:

| Tool | Refund complaints in our sample | Policy group |
|---|---|---|
| Domestika | 16 | 14 days, not on Plus |
| Skillshare | 15 | Narrow exception |
| Base44 | 13, together with billing | No refunds |
| Creatify | 12 | No refunds |
| Crayo | About 9 | No refunds |
| Tripo | About 9, together with cancelling | Narrow exception |
| Jenni AI | 8 | No refunds |
| Submagic | 7 | No refunds |
| JoggAI | 6 | No refunds |

These counts come from small, negative-only samples, so they say nothing about how common problems are overall. What they do show is the shape of the complaint. Most aren't about a refused refund for a bad product. They're about a charge the person didn't expect: an annual plan bought by mistake, a renewal after they thought they'd cancelled, or a trial that turned into a subscription. Base44's sample alone included four people who said they'd bought an annual plan by accident.

## The yearly discount trap

This is the finding we'd most want a buyer to remember. Many of these tools offer large discounts for paying a year up front:

- AhaSlides' monthly price is about three times its yearly rate.
- InVideo and OpusClip offer around 50% off for yearly billing.
- VideoGen saves 33%, which means paying $228 or more up front, under terms that say all purchases are non-refundable.
- Creatify shows a 30% saving and getimg.ai about 26%, with both plans hard to refund.
- Base44 saves 20%, and Vista Social's terms let annual renewals rise by up to 7%. Its annual plans renew unless you cancel at least 30 days ahead.

The discount is real. So is the risk. A year paid up front on a non-refundable plan is a bet that you'll still want the tool in eleven months, made before you've used it in anger. Our rule of thumb from these reviews: pay monthly until you've used the tool for real work for at least a month, and until you've seen how credits actually run down. Then switch to yearly if it's earned it.

## What your rights are anyway

Written terms aren't the whole story. In the EU and the UK, consumers usually have a 14-day right to cancel online purchases. For digital services that right can be lost once you start using the service, if you agreed to that at checkout, which many sign-up flows ask you to do. Jenni AI's terms acknowledge the EU right, and Domestika's terms note mandatory rights in the EEA, UK and Switzerland. Business purchases usually don't get the same protection.

If you're charged for something you didn't agree to, such as a duplicate charge or a renewal after a documented cancellation, most of these policies do allow a correction, and your card issuer's dispute process exists for exactly that. Treat it as a last resort, and keep evidence: screenshots of your cancellation, emails and dates. This isn't legal advice, and rules differ by country.

## A five-step check before you pay

1. **Find the refund rule before the price.** Search the terms for "refund" and the help centre for "cancel". If the two disagree, assume the stricter one applies.
2. **Work out what "used" means.** If the refund needs an unused plan, the free tier is your real trial. Do your testing there.
3. **Pay monthly first.** Skip the yearly discount until a month of real use has earned it.
4. **Diary the renewal date the day you subscribe**, plus any notice period. Vista Social, for example, needs 30 days' notice on annual plans.
5. **Screenshot your cancellation.** Billing disputes in the reviews we read often came down to whether a cancellation went through.

## What this means for our reviews

Refund terms are part of the pricing research behind our reviews, and they count against a tool's value score when they're harsh. A cheap plan you can't get out of is worth less than a slightly dearer one with a fair month to decide. ## FAQ

### Do AI tools usually offer refunds?

Not in our data. Half of 26 tools refused refunds in their written terms, and the rest attached conditions, usually that you hadn't used the product much.

### Which tools had the best refund terms?

Kommodo and Superwhisper, with 30 days on a first purchase. EaseUS and Slido also mention 30 days, but with tighter conditions.

### Is a refund window the same as a free trial?

Rarely. Most windows require that you haven't generated, downloaded or presented anything. Use the free plan to test instead.

### Should I pay yearly for an AI tool?

Only after a month of real use on monthly billing. Yearly discounts are large, but most of these plans can't be refunded.
