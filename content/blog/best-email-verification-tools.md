Most email verifiers do a fine job on easy addresses. The real difference shows up on catch-all domains, where the server accepts everything and a basic check learns nothing. If you sell to businesses, that can cover a big share of your list, and it's where these tools stop looking alike.

Here's the short version, then the reasoning. You can also see the [ranked list with scores](/best/email-verification-tools).

## Quick picks

- **Best overall: ZeroBounce.** The safest choice for mixed lists, with the widest toolkit and the deepest review history.
- **Best for catch-all lists on a budget: BounceBan.** A low price and a verdict on almost every address.
- **Best for scaled B2B outbound: Allegrow.** Strong on catch-alls, with SOC 2 reports on its top plan, but the cost jumps.
- **Cheapest bulk cleaning: MillionVerifier.** Great price for basic hygiene. Keep it away from catch-all lists.

## How we chose

This guide is research-based, without verification runs of our own. We read pricing pages, terms and documentation, Trustpilot profiles and their one-star reviews, and two 2026 benchmarks. We looked at four things:

1. How well each tool handles catch-all and gateway-protected addresses.
2. What it really costs at realistic volumes, including credit expiry and what you pay for.
3. What users complain about, and how recent those complaints are.
4. How much trust the paperwork inspires, from terms to security reports.

Commission didn't enter into it. Everything was checked on November 5, 2026.

## Read this before trusting any benchmark

Every useful benchmark we found was run by a company selling a verifier, and each one's maker finished first.

- **Allegrow's test (July to August 2026):** 10,000 records across 251 catch-all domains. It measured how many of 500 real people each tool found, and how many of 5,000 invented addresses it wrongly passed. Allegrow matched its real people from public LinkedIn data and says it didn't confirm their mailboxes.
- **ZeroBounce's test (July 2026):** 9,901 addresses from hard domain pools. Its engineers settled disputed addresses themselves, and it calls the test self-conducted.

The two tests measure different things, so they can't be stacked. Use them to rule tools out, not to crown a winner.

| Tool | Allegrow test: real people found | Allegrow test: fake passed | Allegrow test: unresolved | ZeroBounce test: accurate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Allegrow | 491 of 500 | 11 | 197 | 82.05% |

Two things stand out. A group of tools can now judge most catch-all addresses, while others leave most of them unresolved. And the cheapest bulk cleaner passed 640 of 5,000 fake addresses on catch-all domains, which is exactly why you shouldn't use it there.

## 1. ZeroBounce: best overall

**Who it's for:** marketing teams with mixed consumer and business lists, and anyone who wants validation and deliverability tools in one account.

ZeroBounce earns the top spot for breadth and steadiness. Beyond validation, it covers email finding, activity data, warm-up, inbox placement tests, and blacklist and DMARC monitoring. On Trustpilot it holds 4.8 from 2,138 reviews with just 2% one-star, though only 64 of those reviews came in the last 12 months. Unknown results are free, which is a real point in its favour.

**The trade-off:** you pay for it. At $9.90 per 1,000 on its entry subscription, it costs nearly three times BounceBan. Billing and credit surprises were the top theme in its first 20 one-star reviews, including one about Email Finder costing more credits than expected.

Pay-as-you-go starts at $39 for 2,000, which is a poor rate for anything but a one-off.

## 2. BounceBan: best for catch-all lists on a budget

**Who it's for:** outbound teams, founders and agencies with B2B lists full of catch-all domains.

BounceBan does one job at a very good price. In Allegrow's test it left a single record of 10,000 unresolved, while finding 460 of 500 real people and passing only 9 fake addresses. Credits never expire, and single checks are free, so testing costs nothing.

**The trade-off:** consumer addresses look like a weak spot. In ZeroBounce's benchmark it scored 96.66% on business domains but 55.25% on Yahoo. Its terms and privacy notice date from 2022 and still cite the defunct Privacy Shield, which a procurement team will flag.

Read our [full BounceBan review](/tools/bounceban), or see [BounceBan vs ZeroBounce](/compare/bounceban-vs-zerobounce).

## 3. Allegrow: best for scaled B2B outbound

**Who it's for:** larger B2B sales teams that need CRM and sequencer integrations, and security paperwork.

Allegrow topped its own test, so take that with care, but it isn't the only signal. It holds about 4.8 on G2 from roughly 60 reviews, with support and accuracy among the praise. It offers primary email detection, domain health tracking, and SOC 2 reports on its Scale Plus and API plans. Its unlimited plan makes costs predictable at high volume.

**The trade-off:** there's little in between. Starter is $99 a month for 5,000 contacts with no native integrations. Scale Plus is $1,340 a month billed annually. G2 reviewers mention limited integrations on lower tiers and a lack of individual logins. Its Trustpilot profile has no reviews, and in ZeroBounce's benchmark it scored 82.05%.

## 4. MillionVerifier: cheapest bulk cleaning

**Who it's for:** anyone cleaning large, older lists on a tight budget, mostly of non-catch-all addresses.

At $449 per million, nothing else here comes close on price. Credits never expire, and it promises refunds on risky results. It holds 4.2 on Trustpilot from 284 reviews, with 80% five-star.

**The trade-off:** accuracy is the recurring complaint. Of its first 20 one-star reviews, 11 said results were wrong, and 7 described bounces after verification. Its site claims 99%+, but Allegrow's catch-all test recorded it passing 640 of 5,000 invented addresses. Use it as a first pass on simple lists, then treat catch-alls separately.

## Why NeverBounce isn't on this list

NeverBounce is one of the best-known names here, so its absence needs an explanation. We left it out for three reasons.

Its catch-all results were the weakest of the big names. It left 9,633 of 10,000 records unresolved in Allegrow's test, and 50.19% unknown in ZeroBounce's. Rivals ran both tests, but they point the same way.

Its Trustpilot score is 1.9 from 32 reviews. That's a small, unclaimed profile, and Trustpilot itself warns that it may not be representative. Still, its first 20 one-star reviews include six about credits expiring or a policy change, often from people who say they bought credits sold as never expiring.

And the price doesn't make up for it. At $8 per 1,000, with credits expiring after 12 months, it costs more than BounceBan.

If your list has few catch-alls and you already use it happily, there's no emergency. We just wouldn't choose it fresh in 2026.

## How to choose

1. **Check your catch-all share.** Run a sample through any tool. If under about 10% is catch-all, a cheap cleaner may be all you need.
2. **Look at who's on your list.** Mostly company domains points to BounceBan or Allegrow. A consumer mix points to ZeroBounce.
3. **Check your paperwork needs.** If you need SOC 2 reports, that narrows things quickly.
4. **Test on your own list.** Take a few thousand addresses, verify them with two tools, and send to the ones they disagree on. Your own bounces beat any benchmark.
5. **Set a rule for risky results** before they reach a sequence, and keep your hard bounce rate under about 3%, the level at which BounceBan's own help centre says to investigate.

Verification only tells you whether an address can receive mail. It won't tell you that the person wants to hear from you, and it doesn't replace permission or a clean sending setup. Our [deliverability checklist](/blog/email-deliverability-checklist) covers the rest.

## FAQ

### What is a catch-all email?

It's an address on a domain that accepts mail for any recipient. The server says yes to everything, so a standard check can't tell if that specific mailbox exists.

### Which email verifier is most accurate?

No neutral test settles it. ZeroBounce and Allegrow each came first in their own benchmarks. BounceBan was close behind on catch-alls in Allegrow's.

### What's the cheapest email verification tool?

Of those we reviewed, MillionVerifier, at $449 per million. For catch-all lists, BounceBan is better value, because a cheap verdict you can't trust isn't a saving.

### Do I need email verification for a small list?

If you collected the list through a confirmed sign-up form, probably not often. For bought, scraped or old lists, yes, before every campaign.

### Do unverified emails hurt deliverability?

High bounce rates can hurt your sender reputation, so sending to bad addresses has a cost beyond the wasted email.
