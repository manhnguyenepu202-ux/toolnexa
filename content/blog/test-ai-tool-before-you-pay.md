**Quick answer:** treat the free plan or trial as your only safe test, because most paid AI plans can't be refunded once you've used them. In seven days you can check what a real task costs, what the free plan hides, where your data goes and how hard it is to leave. If a tool fails any of those, you've lost nothing.

This plan comes from patterns we keep seeing while researching reviews. When buyers get burned, it's rarely because the AI was bad. It's because credits ran out sooner than expected, a limit appeared mid-task, a renewal arrived unannounced, or the refund rules said no. You can check every one of those before you pay.

## Why testing matters more for AI tools

Three things make AI subscriptions riskier than ordinary software.

**Refunds are rare.** In our [study of 26 tools' refund terms](/blog/ai-tool-refund-policies-study), half refused refunds outright, and most of the rest only refunded if you hadn't used the product. So paying to try something is usually paying for good.

**Credits hide the real price.** Plans are sold in credits, minutes or characters, and different actions burn them at very different rates. At getimg.ai, the same plan buys far more cheap images than video. At Fliki, avatars and AI clips use credits much faster than plain narration. The headline price tells you little until you know what your task costs.

**The product changes under you.** AI tools update models, limits and plans often. getimg.ai retired its free plan and, according to reviews, removed custom model training in 2026. A tool you tested six months ago may not be the tool you'd buy today.

## Before day one: three minutes of reading

Before you sign up, open three pages and note three things:

- **The refund rule.** Search the terms for "refund". Note the window and what counts as "used".
- **The free plan limits.** Note them, or note that they aren't published. AhaSlides, for example, doesn't list its free slide limits; the app tells you when you hit them.
- **The date on the legal pages.** Very old terms are a warning sign. Toolsaday's terms are dated 2021, and getimg.ai's 2022, both older than the products they describe.

If you can't find a refund rule or a company name, that's information too.

## The 7-day plan

### Day 1: Run your real task, not the demo

Sign up with a unique password, and skip any yearly offer at checkout. Then do one piece of work you actually need, not the example on the landing page. If it's a writing tool, draft the email you have to send today. If it's a video tool, make the clip you'd post this week.

Note how long it took and how much editing the output needed. That's your baseline.

### Day 2: Measure what a task costs

Check your credit or usage balance before and after each task. Work out the cost of one finished piece: one image, one minute of video, one email. Then multiply by how many you'd make in a month.

This is where most surprises show up. Base44's docs say credits aren't refunded for AI mistakes, so a project that needs several retries costs several times the credits. If retries are normal for your task, count them in.

### Day 3: Push into the limits

Do the task at the size you'd really use. A longer video, a bigger audience, a heavier file. Free plans often fail here, and quietly. Kommodo's free recordings disappear for viewers after 60 days. AhaSlides drops a free session to a small test audience if your deck goes over the hidden slide limits.

Find out where the wall is now, not in front of your audience.

### Day 4: Check where your data goes

Read the privacy policy and any data controls, and look for three answers: is your content used to train AI models, where is it stored, and can you delete it? Answers vary a lot. AhaSlides says user data doesn't train AI. Context.dev's terms let it train on your content unless you use zero data retention. Wispr Flow processes all dictation in the cloud and has settings you should change on day one.

Also check for a recent security notice. AhaSlides disclosed a breach in September 2026 affecting account and invoice data. A tool that tells you openly is better than one that doesn't, but you should know before you upload anything sensitive.

### Day 5: Try to get help

Ask support one real question, ideally about billing or a limit you hit. Time the reply. Support is the lowest of our four sub-scores for 39 of the 59 tools we've scored, and day five is the cheapest moment to find out how it treats you.

### Day 6: Find the exit

Find the cancel button and the export option, without using them. Note how many clicks it takes, whether cancelling ends access at once or at the end of the period, and what happens to your files. Some tools are generous. Others aren't: OpusClip's help centre warns that projects go out of reach three days after you cancel.

If you can't find how to cancel, stop here. Trouble cancelling comes up again and again in the one-star reviews we read, for tools like Jenni AI, Tripo and Creatify.

### Day 7: Decide, with numbers

Put your notes into one line: "One [task] costs about [X] credits, so a month of my work needs the [Y] plan at [$Z]." Then compare that with one alternative. Often it's a general chatbot you already pay for. ChatGPT drafts emails as well as most dedicated email generators, for example.

If the tool still wins, buy the monthly plan, not yearly.

## After you pay: the first month

- **Diary the renewal date and any notice period** on the day you subscribe.
- **Check usage weekly.** Credits rarely roll over, so plan to use them, and don't buy a bigger plan than you need.
- **Keep receipts and screenshots** of your plan and any cancellation.
- **Switch to yearly only after a month** of real use, if the tool has earned it.

## Red flags that should end the test

- No company name or address in the terms.
- Legal pages years older than the product.
- Free plan limits you can't find anywhere.
- A refund promise on the pricing page that the terms contradict.
- No way to export your work.
- Credits charged for failed generations, with no route to get them back.

One red flag isn't always a dealbreaker. Two or more, and we'd keep looking.

## A simple scorecard

| Check | Pass | Fail |
|---|---|---|
| Real task done well | Little editing needed | Rewrote most of it |
| Monthly cost at your volume | Fits the budget | Needs a bigger plan |
| Limits | Found and acceptable | Hidden or hit mid-task |
| Data use | No training, clear deletion | Trains on content, or unclear |
| Support | Useful reply within 2 days | No reply |
| Exit | Easy cancel and export | Hidden or lossy |

Five passes or more, and it's worth paying monthly. Three or fewer, and the free plan was the right amount to spend.

## FAQ

### Is a free trial enough to judge an AI tool?

Usually, if you use it on real work and check cost, limits, data and cancelling. A demo task tells you very little.

### Should I start on monthly or yearly billing?

Monthly. Yearly discounts are large, but most AI plans can't be refunded once used.

### What if there's no free plan?

Buy the smallest monthly plan as a paid trial, and keep within any refund condition while you test. getimg.ai, for example, refunds only if you've used fewer than 100 credits within 3 days.

### How do I know what a task really costs?

Check your balance before and after one finished piece of work, include any retries, and multiply by your monthly volume.
