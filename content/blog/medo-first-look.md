MeDo promises what every AI app builder promises: describe an app in plain English, and get a working product with accounts, a database and a live link. Its documentation is unusually detailed, which helps. Reviews from real users, though, are almost missing.

## Why this is a first look, not a review

We haven't scored MeDo. Trustpilot has just three reviews, Product Hunt has no reviews page at the address we checked, and the third-party articles we found mostly repeat marketing or list conflicting prices. Without real user feedback, any score would be a guess. We'll publish a full review when that changes.

## How we evaluated this

This is research only, without building an app of our own. We read MeDo's credit system, billing, FAQ, about, terms and privacy pages, its Trustpilot profile, and third-party directory write-ups. MeDo's pricing page didn't load for our reader, so prices come from its documentation, which says the in-app billing page is authoritative. Everything was checked on November 5, 2026.

## What MeDo is

MeDo generates web and mobile apps from a conversation, including the front end, login, backend and database. You publish to a MeDo link, connect a custom domain on paid plans, and can link tools such as Stripe, Google Sheets, Slack and Notion.

The company behind it is SAILAI PRIVATE LIMITED, registered in Singapore. Several directories describe MeDo as a Baidu product, but none of the official pages we read mention Baidu, so we can't confirm that link.

## Who it's for

On paper, MeDo suits founders and small businesses that want a simple internal tool, booking app or landing page without hiring a developer. It looks less suited to apps with complex logic, or to anyone who needs firm guarantees about support. Like every tool in this category, you'd still want someone to review what it builds, and MeDo's terms say the same.

## Pricing: what you'll really pay

| Plan | Monthly | Yearly | Subscription credits | Daily login bonus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Free | $0 | $0 | None | 80 a day, up to 400 a month |
| Pro | $20 | $200 | 2,000 | 100 a day, up to 3,000 a month |
| Max | $50 | $500 | 6,000 | 100 a day, up to 3,000 a month |

Pricing checked November 2026, from MeDo's documentation.

The headline credits are only half the story. Here are the costs that matter:

| Action | Credits |
|---|---|
| Deep Build message (the default) | 40 |
| Fast Build message | 10 |
| Publish an app | 20 |
| Backend, per app, per day | 15, 30, 50 or 300 |
| Download source code | 300 |
| Package for iOS | 500 |

That daily backend charge is the part most buyers will miss. An app on the lowest backend tier uses about 450 credits a month just by staying on, and Medium uses about 900. If credits run out, the backend pauses. Using MeDo's $20 pack for 2,000 credits, 100 credits cost about $1, so that's roughly $4.50 to $9 a month per app, on top of building.

Here's how Pro's 2,000 subscription credits could go, ignoring login bonuses:

| Monthly work | Credits | Left of 2,000 |
|---|---|---|
| 30 Deep Build messages, publish, no backend | 1,220 | 780 |
| Same, plus one Low backend | 1,670 | 330 |
| Two apps on Medium backends, no building | 1,800 | 200 |

So running a couple of apps can use most of Pro before you change a thing. Fast Build at a quarter of the price helps, and the login bonus adds a real cushion if you sign in daily.

Some useful rules: credits return when a generation fails, left-over subscription credits carry over for one month, and pack credits last 365 days. On the other hand, all fees are non-refundable except where the law requires, or where a charge was made in error.

## What users complain about

There isn't enough to call a pattern. Trustpilot shows a 3.4 score from three reviews: two five-star, and one one-star from October 2026. The positive reviews praise ease of use and credit allowances compared with rivals. The negative one describes unanswered support, a monthly charge after credits expired, and a refused request to move project data. One review proves little, but those are exactly the things that matter when you depend on a hosted app.

## Things to check before paying

- **Getting your app out.** Source download needs Pro, and costs 300 credits each time. Check how your data moves too, as the terms don't clearly say who owns generated code, though the FAQ says your data is yours.
- **Data use.** Your prompts go to third-party AI providers. The only explicit no-training promise we found covers Google-connected data.
- **Free apps sleep.** A free plan backend pauses after five inactive days.
- **Support.** Help runs through email and Discord, with priority support on Max.

## Early verdict

MeDo publishes its costs more openly than many rivals, and that deserves credit. Used on Fast Build, with a small backend, it could be a cheap way to test an idea. But judge it on running cost, not build cost, and export your project before you rely on it. Start on the free plan, and upgrade only once you know what your app uses each day.

## FAQ

### Is MeDo free?

Yes, there's a free plan with 100 sign-up credits and daily login credits. Paid plans remove the "by Medo" badge and add custom domains.

### How much does it cost to run a MeDo app?

Backends cost 15 to 300 credits per app per day. The lowest tier uses about 450 credits a month.

### Can I export my MeDo app?

Yes, on Pro and above. Each source code download costs 300 credits.

### Can I get a refund?

The terms say fees are non-refundable, except where the law requires, or where a charge was made in error.

There are only three Trustpilot reviews and no reliable independent tests yet.
