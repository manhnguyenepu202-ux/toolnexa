Choosing an email platform feels simple until you are three years in, paying five times what you expected, and stuck with automations you can't migrate. This guide gives you a short framework to make the right call the first time.

## 1. Start with your business model, not the feature list

Every email tool claims automation, templates and analytics. The real differences show up when you match a platform to **how you make money**:

- **E-commerce store** — you need purchase data, abandoned-cart flows and product recommendations. Look at [Klaviyo](/tools/klaviyo) first.
- **Creator or course seller** — tagging, simple funnels and selling digital products matter most. Look at [Kit](/tools/kit) or [beehiiv](/tools/beehiiv).
- **B2B or service business** — lead scoring, CRM and conditional automations pay off. Look at [ActiveCampaign](/tools/activecampaign).
- **Small business newsletter** — a clean editor and a low price win. Look at [MailerLite](/tools/mailerlite) or [Brevo](/tools/brevo).

## 2. Understand how pricing scales

Most platforms charge by **contacts stored**. A few, like Brevo, charge by **emails sent**. The difference is huge once your list grows:

- If you have a large list but email only a few times a month, send-based pricing is usually cheaper.
- If you email a small, engaged list very often, contact-based pricing can be fine.

Before you sign up, price each shortlisted tool at **three list sizes**: today, 12 months from now, and your two-year goal. The cheapest tool today is often not the cheapest at 25,000 contacts.

## 3. Check deliverability tools, not deliverability promises

No vendor can guarantee inbox placement — that depends mostly on your sending habits. What a good platform *can* do is make best practice easy:

- Guided setup of SPF, DKIM and DMARC on your own domain
- One-click unsubscribe headers
- Bounce and complaint handling, plus clear reporting

Read our [email deliverability checklist](/blog/email-deliverability-checklist) for the full list.

## 4. Test the automation builder with a real workflow

Sign up for a trial and build one real automation, for example a five-email welcome sequence that branches when someone clicks a link. You will learn more in 30 minutes than from any comparison table:

1. Is the builder visual and easy to follow?
2. Can you branch on behaviour (opens, clicks, purchases, tags)?
3. Can you see where subscribers are inside the flow?

## 5. Think about migration before you commit

Moving platforms is painful: automations rarely export, and you may need to re-warm your sending reputation. Pick a tool you can grow with for at least two to three years.
